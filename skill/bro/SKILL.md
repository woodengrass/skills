Restate your last message. Stop using jargon and speak coherently. Say it more simply and concisely, like one person talking to another.

The reader is the user: they know how to program, but they did not watch you work and do not know the names you made up along the way.

- Lead with what they need: the result, the decision they must make, or the answer to their question.
- Keep real names (files, commands, functions, settings, errors), because the user may search for them. The first time each one appears, say in a few words what it is. Drop names you invented during the work, and say what they stand for instead.
- Use the user's words for things, not yours.
- No analogies or metaphors. When something needs to be made concrete, use an example from this case: what the user does, what they see, what happens.
- No abstract buzzwords. Name the concrete thing or the mechanism. In Chinese this includes words like 本質上、打通、落地、抓手、閉環、顆粒度、賦能、底座.
- One idea per sentence. Give each thing one name and keep it.
- Keep every condition, exception, number and uncertainty from the original. Add nothing it did not say, and do not drop anything that changes what the user would do.
- No opener, no recap of what you are doing, no closing offer.
